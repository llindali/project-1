# Linda’s confirmed assumption review
Confirmed by Linda in this conversation on October 6, 2026. Codex organized and corrected the wording; this is a post-AI review, not an AI-off prediction or pre-AI reflection.

## Decision and judgment
The investment committee is deciding whether to initiate, watch/defer, or decline an equity position in Eli Lilly. The valuation date is September 8, 2026, with a $1,123.91 closing-price reference.

I agree with watch/defer. The approximately $754 base estimate is 32.9% below the reference price; the conditional downside/upside scenarios are approximately $398 and $1,228, or -64.6% and +9.3%. These scenarios have no assigned probabilities. They indicate insufficient margin of safety under the current assumptions. I would reconsider after stronger evidence supports sustainable growth, cash conversion and the discount rate, or after a lower entry price with the operating thesis intact.

Holding other assumptions fixed, one reverse-DCF solution adds 9.62 percentage points to each 2027–2031 growth rate and reaches $260.9bn in 2031 revenue, versus $174.6bn in the base. This is not the market’s unique forecast.

## Assumptions I would defend or investigate
- Revenue growth (25%, 20%, 15%, 10%, 7%): need more evidence. Check realized net pricing, prescription volume versus channel inventory, patient persistence, competition, product-specific clinical and regulatory milestones. Stronger sustainable growth could narrow the valuation gap; slower growth reinforces deferral.
- Illustrative 9% WACC: need more evidence and calibration. Verify dated Treasury yield, equity risk premium, beta definition/period, market-value capital weights and after-tax borrowing costs. Hypothetical 4% risk-free rate, 5% premium and beta 0.65–0.85 imply cost of equity of 7.25%–8.25%, not a measured Lilly WACC. Changing only model WACC to 8% gives $911.15/share, still 18.9% below reference; this alone does not justify initiation.
- Recurring R&D at 20% plus $4bn acquired IPR&D: provisionally accept as an explicit assumption, pending historical spending, transaction and pipeline checks. Acquired IPR&D expense is not automatically acquisition cash spending. Do not assume spending cuts improve durable business value.
- Capex taper to 8% and 330 inventory days: need more evidence. Check product-specific capacity, site validation, yields, inventory conversion and cash spending. The model does not link capex directly to physical capacity or EBIT margins. Persistent working-capital drag reinforces deferral.
- Terminal growth 2.5% and incremental ROIC 20%: need more evidence. These are long-run assumptions, not established GDP growth or guaranteed patent moats. The 78.4% terminal share is an output. Test a longer explicit forecast or gradual fade in growth and returns; review patent and competitive risks. High terminal dependence limits confidence in the point estimate.
- Shares and claims: need more evidence. The 893.7m diluted-average share count and June balances are proxies. Verify spot dilution and financial claims/nonoperating assets without double-counting operating liabilities. No particular convertible, warrant, pension deficit or numerical dilution effect is assumed without evidence.

## Conditions for reconsideration
A proposed 15%–20% discount to the current base implies approximately $603–$641, conditional on fundamentals remaining intact. This is my proposed policy, not a confirmed committee requirement. Stronger throughput and inventory conversion without pricing erosion could support revisiting the operating forecast. Clinical/regulatory progress warrants reassessment; lower COGS or expanded access require separate economic evidence.

## Material limits
Aggregate revenue growth does not model patient/product demand or patent cliffs. Capex has no direct capacity feedback. Remaining-2026 cash-flow proration may misstate within-year timing; excluding already-paid acquisitions avoids counting past payments again. WACC and terminal assumptions materially affect value. Approximate shares, dated balances and simplified acquired-asset accounting constrain precision.
