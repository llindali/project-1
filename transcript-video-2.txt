Hi, I’m Linda Li. This video explains how the Eli Lilly valuation model runs, how its financial statements connect, and how it's calculations were validated.

The core program requires Python 3.10 or newer and uses the standard library. It does not require an API key or third-party packages to run the valuation and tests. PDF regeneration is separate and uses ReportLab.

[In PowerShell, run: python model.py]

The model includes a 2026 bridge and five full forecast years, 2027 through 2031.

[Show legacy_statements.py, then model.py.]

The inherited statement engine projects the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. The integrated model converts those operating forecasts into free cash flow to the firm.

Working capital connects revenue and operating costs to cash requirements. Capital expenditure and depreciation feed the asset roll-forward.

Free cash flow starts with operating income, subtracts unlevered cash taxes, adds depreciation and amortization, and subtracts capital expenditure, changes in operating working capital, and applicable capitalized acquisitions.

Purchased research already deducted from operating income is not deducted a second time. Negative explicit free cash flow is retained.

[Show the value function in model.py.]

The model discounts operating cash flows and terminal value to estimate enterprise value. It then adds cash and investments, subtracts debt, and divides equity value by the diluted-average share proxy.

The base uses an illustrative 9% WACC, 2.5% terminal growth, and 20% incremental terminal return on invested capital as assumptions.

[In PowerShell, run: python -m unittest -v. Show the final result.]

The tests check statement articulation, valuation identities, directional behavior, and failure conditions.

A synthetic known-answer example produces $8.20 per common share. Other checks test whether higher WACC reduces value, whether the reverse DCF reproduces the reference price, and whether invalid inputs or corrupted accounting are rejected.

Passing these checks supports mechanical consistency. It does not establish that the forecast is economically correct.

[Show output/rd-assisted-test-result.json.]

The documented R&D sensitivity changes recurring research from 20% to 17% of revenue. The verified value rises from $754.19 to $810.23, below my predicted range of $820 to $880. Watch/defer remains the recommendation.

Seventeen percent falls outside the ordinary model’s 18% to 24% range. The separate exploratory script documents the initial refusal and a temporary range extension; the production safeguards remain unchanged.

The remaining limitations include approximate dilution, an illustrative discount rate, high terminal-value dependence, and no explicit feedback from research or capacity spending into future business success.

My conclusion is that the model provides a reproducible framework for challenging assumptions, while the investment recommendation still depends on evidence and judgment.
